# Course Categorization: KTH Applied and Computational Mathematics MSc

This document categorizes courses in the KTH Applied and Computational Mathematics master's programme across two dimensions:
*   **Autodidactal (High/Low):** High means the material is fast to learn with specific documentation and project experience. Low means it requires all-around conceptual understanding and deliberate theoretical studying.
*   **Industrial (High/Low):** High means the skills are directly applicable and in demand in quantitative/engineering industry roles. Low implies an academic or theoretical focus.

## 2x2 Strategic Matrix

| | High Industrial (Employable) | Low Industrial (Academic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Low Autodidactal** <br>*(Hard Theory)* | **The Core (Competitive Moat)**<br>• SF2822 Applied Nonlinear Optimization<br>• SF2942 Portfolio Theory and Risk Management<br>• SF2955 Computer Intensive Methods | **The PhD Prep**<br>• SF2940 Probability Theory (Measure-theoretic)<br>• SF2739 Partial Differential Equations<br>• SF2743 Advanced Real Analysis |
| **High Autodidactal** <br>*(Fast Tech/Tools)* | **The Portfolio Builder**<br>• SF2565 C++ for Scientific Computing<br>• SF2935 Modern Methods of Statistical Learning<br>• DD2421 Machine Learning | **The Filler**<br>• AK2030 Theory and Methodology of Science<br>• Mandatory seminar/reading courses |

---
**Strategy summary:** The greatest long-term advantage comes from doubling down on **Low Autodidactal + High Industrial** courses to build an intellectual moat, while using **High Autodidactal + High Industrial** courses to balance your workload and build a tangible project portfolio.
