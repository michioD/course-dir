# Evaluation for SF2930: Regression Analysis (7.5 ECTS)

### Course Evaluation: SF2930 Regression Analysis (7.5 ECTS)

#### 1. "Autodidact" Threshold
* **Verdict:** **FAIL**
* **Rationale:** The course covers standard applied linear models, Generalized Linear Models (GLMs), diagnostics, regularized regression (Lasso/Ridge), and variable selection implemented in R. While it involves linear algebra and distribution theory, the core material consists of standard statistical workflows and packages widely documented across accessible textbooks, online courses, and official documentation. It does not require high-density pen-and-paper mathematical proofs that present substantial barriers to self-study.

---

#### 2. Transferability
* **Verdict:** **High**
* **Rationale:** Regression modeling, residual diagnostics, hypothesis testing, and GLMs serve as foundational modeling techniques across almost all data-driven disciplines, including machine learning, quantitative finance, biostatistics, econometrics, and industrial engineering.

---

#### 3. Prerequisite Knowledge
* **Verdict:** **High**
* **Rationale:** A solid grasp of linear models, residual behavior, and inference is an indispensable stepping stone ("map/moat") for modern statistical learning and econometrics, yet this course only covers foundational tools. The broader field has extensive depth (non-parametrics, causal inference, high-dimensional statistics, deep learning), meaning the course provides a high ceiling baseline without covering 80% of modern advanced statistical modeling.

---

#### 4. Frequently Used on the Job
* **Verdict:** **Moderate**
* **Rationale:** While standard linear and logistic regression models or their diagnostic intuitions are used regularly by data scientists and quantitative analysts, many modern software/ML engineering roles rely on black-box ML frameworks (e.g., gradient boosting, neural networks) rather than formal classical regression diagnostics on a daily basis.

---

### Strategic Takeaway
SF2930 is a highly transferable foundational course that provides essential baseline statistical intuition, but its standard applied curriculum makes it easily self-teachable outside a formal university setting.
