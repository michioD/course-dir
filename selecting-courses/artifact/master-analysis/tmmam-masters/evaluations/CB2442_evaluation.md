# Evaluation for CB2442: Bioinformatics (7.5 ECTS)

### Course Evaluation: CB2442 Bioinformatics (7.5 ECTS)

#### 1. "Autodidact" Threshold: **FAIL**
* **Rationale:** The course focuses heavily on standard applied bioinformatic tooling, command-line utilities (Unix/Linux, regular expressions), public biological databases (NCBI, Ensembl, UniProt), web platforms (Galaxy), and running established algorithms (BLAST, basic pairwise/multiple sequence alignment, vector clustering). It lacks deep pen-and-paper theoretical proofs or heavy mathematical rigor, making the majority of the material readily self-teachable via official documentation, GitHub repositories, and online tutorials.

---

#### 2. Transferability: **Moderate**
* **Rationale:** While core biological concepts (phylogeny, gene expression analysis, sequence homology) are domain-specific to biotechnology and life sciences, the underlying data analysis methods—such as string matching heuristics, distance metrics, hierarchical clustering, Unix scripting, and data extraction pipelines—have direct utility in adjacent data analysis and engineering domains.

---

#### 3. Prerequisite Knowledge: **High**
* **Rationale:** The course serves as a foundational gateway into computational biology. However, modern bioinformatics is an evolving, deep field (involving high-throughput next-generation sequencing, structural modeling, and deep learning architectures like AlphaFold). This course acts as an initial "map" covering basic concepts, but it covers well under 20% of the depth required for advanced research or modern computational biology engineering.

---

#### 4. Frequently Used on the Job: **High**
* **Rationale:** For bioinformatics engineers and computational biologists, the topics covered (sequence querying, alignments, BLAST execution, FASTA/FASTQ handling, regex parsing, expression analysis, and database retrieval) are essential, non-negotiable core skills applied on a daily basis.

---

### Strategic Takeaway
CB2442 delivers highly practical, day-to-day foundational competencies for biotech and life-science workflows, but its tool-centric and applied nature means the material can be learned independently without university instruction.
