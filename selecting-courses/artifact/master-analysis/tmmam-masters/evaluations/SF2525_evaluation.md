# Evaluation for SF2525: Computational Methods for SDEs and Machine Learning (7.5 ECTS)

### Course Evaluation: SF2525 – Computational Methods for SDEs and Machine Learning (7.5 ECTS)

#### 1. "Autodidact" Threshold: PASS
- **Rationale:** The course combines stochastic calculus (Itô calculus, Euler-Maruyama, Milstein schemes, weak/strong convergence analysis) with high-dimensional PDEs and machine learning approaches (e.g., deep BSDE solvers, physics-informed neural networks, score-based diffusion models). The underlying numerical analysis, stability guarantees, and mathematical rigor make self-learning difficult without a formal foundation in advanced numerical analysis and probability theory.

#### 2. Transferability: Moderate
- **Rationale:** The mathematical and computational frameworks apply well across specific quantitative fields—primarily quantitative finance (derivative pricing, risk modeling), computational physics/chemistry (molecular dynamics), and generative modeling in machine learning (diffusion/score-based models). Outside of these mathematically intensive modeling domains, the specific methods have limited direct applicability in general software engineering or standard machine learning workflows.

#### 3. Prerequisite Knowledge: High
- **Rationale:** SDEs and high-dimensional stochastic computing feature a steep learning curve and a very high skill ceiling. A 7.5 ECTS introductory/master’s course serves as a foundational map rather than exhaustive coverage, as mastery requires deep background in measure-theoretic probability, PDE theory, and ongoing research in generative ML and high-dimensional scientific computing.

#### 4. Frequently Used on the Job: Low to Moderate
- **Rationale:** 
  - **Low** for general ML engineers, data scientists, and software engineers, where standard libraries abstract away underlying stochastic numerics.
  - **Moderate** for specialized quantitative researchers (quant finance) and core generative AI researchers working on continuous-time diffusion models, though day-to-day work still heavily relies on established high-level frameworks.

---

### Strategic Takeaway
SF2525 is a high-rigor, mathematically dense course that builds a strong technical moat for specialized domains (quantitative finance, scientific ML, and generative diffusion research), but its specific tools and theorems are rarely utilized in standard mainstream engineering roles.
