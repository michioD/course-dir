# Evaluation for SF2956: Topological Data Analysis (7.5 ECTS)

### Evaluation: SF2956 Topological Data Analysis (7.5 ECTS)

---

#### 1. "Autodidact" Threshold
* **Verdict:** **PASS**
* **Rationale:** The course relies heavily on abstract mathematical foundations, including algebraic topology, simplicial complexes, homology, persistence modules, and the stability theorem for persistence diagrams. The theoretical density, formal definitions, and proof-heavy concepts (e.g., functoriality, algebraic invariants) are difficult to self-teach effectively compared to standard machine learning pipelines.

---

#### 2. Transferability
* **Verdict:** **Moderate**
* **Rationale:** TDA provides invariant feature representations that apply across complex, high-dimensional datasets with non-Euclidean structures—such as computational biology (protein structures, genomics), material science, time-series/financial regime-change detection, and geometric deep learning. However, it remains a specialized structural tool rather than a universally applicable data analysis standard.

---

#### 3. Prerequisite Knowledge & Depth
* **Verdict:** **High**
* **Rationale:** A single introductory course introduces simplicial homology and basic persistent homology algorithms, but the field extends deeply into multiparameter persistence, categorical topology, and geometric measure theory. It provides foundational mathematical machinery while leaving a high ceiling for advanced research and custom metric design.

---

#### 4. Frequently Used on the Job
* **Verdict:** **Low**
* **Rationale:** In industry data science and machine learning roles, standard gradient-boosted trees, standard deep neural networks, and classical dimensionality reduction dominate daily workflows. TDA tools (e.g., `Gudhi`, `Ripser`) are primarily utilized in specialized R&D roles (e.g., biopharma structural modeling) rather than as core daily tools across broader engineering positions.

---

### Strategic Takeaway
SF2956 functions as a high-rigor mathematical moat that introduces non-standard geometric and topological perspectives on data, but its practical application remains confined to specialized research niches rather than standard industry workflows.
