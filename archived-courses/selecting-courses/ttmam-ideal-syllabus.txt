My Ideal TTMAM Syllabus — transferable technical moat

Objective

Build knowledge that is genuinely expensive to acquire alone: mathematical modelling,
uncertainty, numerical optimization, feedback systems, and rigorous model evaluation.
These are useful in robotics, quantitative finance, ML and computational biotech. The
target is not four shallow specialisations; it is one strong technical foundation with
finance as an applied proving ground.

Selection rule

Prefer courses with difficult theory, labs/projects, feedback, or specialised equipment.
Do not spend scarce credits on tools that can be learned later from documentation. In
particular, a second generic deep-learning course is lower priority than systems,
optimization, probability, and a substantial robotics project.

Fixed programme courses

- SF2940 Probability Theory, P1, 7.5 hp — mandatory. The common language of risk,
  Bayesian inference, filtering, RL and stochastic biological models.
- AK2030 + SA2001, P1, 7.5 hp total — mandatory methodology courses.

The ten elective courses

1. SF2524 Matrix Computations for Large-scale Systems, P2, 7.5 hp
   Required programme breadth and a durable computational skill: sparse/iterative linear
   algebra sits underneath simulation, optimisation, computer vision, genomics and large
   ML systems.

2. SF2957 Statistical Machine Learning, P2, 7.5 hp
   The statistical discipline behind prediction, uncertainty and model evaluation. More
   transferable than framework-specific ML training.

3. DD2437 Artificial Neural Networks and Deep Architectures, P3, 7.5 hp
   Keep one modern representation-learning course. Use its work to become fluent in
   experimental design, training diagnostics and reproducible implementation—not merely
   a user of a library.

4. SF2812 Applied Linear Optimization, P3, 7.5 hp
   Learn to formulate and solve constrained allocation and decision problems. It is a
   practical complement to nonlinear optimization and transfers to portfolios, planning,
   resource allocation and experimental design.

5. SF2822 Applied Nonlinear Optimization, P4, 7.5 hp
   A core optimisation course: constrained problems, Newton/SQP/interior-point methods
   and modelling judgement. Directly useful for control, portfolio construction, ML and
   parameter estimation in biological models.

6. SF2701 Financial Mathematics, Basic Course, P4, 7.5 hp
   The finance foundation: no-arbitrage modelling, stochastic thinking and derivatives.
   Together with Portfolio Theory it makes finance a serious second application rather
   than a superficial career option.

7. SF2852 Optimal Control Theory, Y2 P1, 7.5 hp
   Dynamic programming, HJB, Pontryagin, LQ control, MPC and numerical optimal control.
   This is the strongest single bridge between robotics, RL, operations research and
   dynamical biotech models.

8. SF2942 Portfolio Theory and Risk Management, Y2 P1, 7.5 hp
   Gives the finance proving ground: risk, dependence, optimisation under uncertainty and
   communicating model limits. It makes the mathematics legible to quant employers.

9. SF2832 Mathematical Systems Theory, Y2 P2, 7.5 hp
   The structural backbone: state-space models, linear dynamical systems, controllability,
   observability and signal-system reasoning. It transfers to tracking, time series,
   stochastic processes and systems biology.

10. EL2805 Reinforcement Learning, Y2 P2, 7.5 hp
    Take RL after probability, statistical ML, optimisation and control—not as a first
    exposure. At that point it becomes a rigorous connection between sequential decision
    making, control and finance rather than a collection of algorithms.

Why this is the right shape

- Robotics: numerical linear algebra -> learning -> systems/control -> sequential
  decision-making. This is the durable mathematical substrate for perception, planning
  and robot learning.
- Finance: probability -> statistical learning -> nonlinear optimisation -> portfolio
  risk; control and RL add sequential-decision depth.
- ML: linear algebra -> statistical learning -> neural networks -> optimisation -> RL.
- Biotech: the same pipeline applies to noisy biological data, inverse/state-estimation
  problems, dynamical models and constrained parameter fitting.

Important checks and substitutions

- This is an academically feasible 90 hp coursework plan: 15 hp fixed courses + ten
  7.5 hp electives. It does not require an overloaded P1.
- DD2419 Project Course in Robotics and Autonomous Systems is a very strong practical
  option, but it requires DD2410 (or an accepted equivalent). TTMAM's mandatory P1 load
  already occupies 15 hp, while DD2410 is P1. Take DD2419 only if you have the recognised
  equivalent before the programme, receive a prerequisite exception, or deliberately
  agree an alternative study plan with the programme director. It cannot be honestly
  presented as a free add-on to this schedule.
- DD2603 Geometric Robot Learning is an excellent elective, but it is not in the core ten:
  it competes in P2 with the compulsory/transferable matrix + statistical-ML foundation
  and requires completed basic ML. Swap it in only if you already have the ML prerequisite
  and are deliberately prioritising robot-learning research. The cleanest swap is for
  DD2437, not for SF2524, SF2957, SF2822, SF2832 or SF2852.
- EL2320 Applied Estimation is the best robotics/biotech substitute if you decide not to
  take DD2437. Kalman/particle filtering is more cross-domain and less self-teachable
  than another deep-learning survey, but it also competes in P2.
- If biotech becomes a firm target rather than an option, replace SF2942 with DD2435
  Mathematical Modelling of Biological Systems. Keep SF2942 when finance remains a
  serious career path.

Deliberately not prioritised

- Quantum computing/neural networks: interesting, but currently much weaker transfer to
  the four target areas than control, estimation and optimisation.
- Formal methods, cryptography and parallel computing: valuable specialisations, but they
  do not beat the selected foundation unless you decide to target safety-critical robotics,
  security, or HPC systems specifically.
- Martingales and stochastic integrals: take only if you choose a stochastic-calculus or
  derivatives-heavy quant thesis. Portfolio/risk is the better broad finance course.
